What happened

  1. A clean opening. JJ played the Sicilian and for thirteen moves the game was dead level — never worse than a tenth of a pawn. Nothing to fix here.
  2. The opponent gave a pawn away. 15.Qc3+ was a check that did nothing, and 16.Rae1 ignored the loose pawn on d5.
  3. JJ took it. 16…Nxd5! — Stockfish's first choice. A pawn up and clearly better.
  4. Good defending. 18…e6 shut the door on the c4–f7 diagonal just as the queen and knight lined up on f7.
  5. Then it slipped away. 24…Nxe4 and especially 25…d5?! handed the extra pawn back. After 26.exd5 the position opened and the advantage went from +1.0 to level.

Two of Coach Li's rules, live in this game

  • Never trade just because you can. When you are a pawn up, every trade helps the person who is behind. 24…Nxe4 was that trade.
  • Before you push a pawn, check your pieces are safe. 25…d5 was the push that let everything out.
