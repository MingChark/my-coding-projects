🗣️ What Teacher Bowen said

1
Bars 1–4 · the right-hand rule
📅 25 Oct 2026 · 0:21 and again at 2:58
“Every time you're playing the two notes together — if it's on the first string, you're going to use ring. If it's on the second, you're going to use the middle.”

One rule for the whole opening: a (ring) for anything on the first string, m (middle) for anything on the second. That is exactly what Jason wrote at the top of the page — ring ↑, middle ↓ — and the printed a i a i a i over bar 1 is the pattern it produces.

Bars 1–4. The circled a and i above the notes are the right-hand fingers.
2
Bars 1–7 · the open G has to be quiet
📅 25 Oct 2026 · 3:28
“This time I want you to play all the open G quietly. All of them. Your index finger is going to be very quiet — but not the other notes. Only your index finger playing that open G.”

The open G is the note that keeps coming back underneath the tune — it is the i finger every time. It is meant to sit under the melody, not next to it. Teacher Bowen made Jason play the line four times over; each time it crept back up and he stopped him and asked for it again.

Then a check: “just touch, so you're relaxing here — is your hand hurting?” Quiet comes from a relaxed hand, not from a careful one.

Bars 5–7 — the same pattern carrying on.
3
Bar 8 on · a new pattern — middle first
📅 25 Oct 2026 · 4:35 and 5:32
“You're going to start with your middle finger, right? m, i, m, i, m. … Middle first — you're doing index first.”

At bar 8 the right hand changes. Each group is five notes: m i m i m, and it starts on m. Jason kept starting on i out of habit from the first line — Teacher Bowen stopped him on it three separate times.

4
Bar 8 on · those basses are E, not A
📅 25 Oct 2026 · 6:10 — and he asked for it in writing
“Just make a note for yourself on the paper. One, circle the bass notes — those are E's, not A's. And two, circle the m, i, m, i — just to remind yourself.”

This is the one piece of written homework in the whole lesson, and it is the correction he came back to most: Jason was reading the low notes a string too low. They are E. You can see the circles he drew round them on the page above, with E pencilled underneath.

Bars 8–12. The circled bass notes, and E written under each one.
5
Bars 16–18 · two fast, two not
📅 25 Oct 2026 · 9:14
“For those four notes, we have two sixteenths — faster. And then the last two eighths are not faster. … Last two notes a little bit slower.”

A rhythm trap, not a notes trap. The group looks even on the page but it isn't: the first two are twice as quick as the last two. The easy mistake is to let the whole group run at one speed.

Bars 16–20. The position marks VI V IV V are the ones in note 6.
6
Bars 19–20 · the shape that slides
📅 25 Oct 2026 · 9:42 → 11:10 — the longest stretch of the lesson
“You have a Roman numeral telling you it's the sixth position. … Your index finger is going to be at the sixth fret.”
“You see the number in a circle? It's trying to tell you which string it is. … Second. … And the other note is on the fifth.”
“So it's F and F. And then you're just going to move this whole thing down a fret — and then down another fret — and then go back up a fret.”

This is the hardest bar on the page and it is really one shape moved three times. Hold the two notes on the ② and ⑤ strings, index at the 6th fret, and walk it: F → E → D♯ → E. That is exactly what the printed VI · V · IV · V above the bars means, and it matches the note names Jason pencilled in underneath.

Two asides while they drilled it: “use your ring” for the lower note, and — when the shape wasn't pressed cleanly — “that sounds a little crunchy… that sounds better.”

Bars 19–20 up close: ② and ⑤ are the strings, VI V IV V the positions, and F · E · D♯ · E the notes.
7
Bars 21–23 · read it, don't guess it
📅 25 Oct 2026 · 11:38 → 13:00
“Now it's different after this. Figure out the note. These notes you should know very easily. … F is not sharp in this case.”
“Look at the book — it wants you to use 3–4 for your fingering. That's 2–3.”

Two separate habits caught in a row. First, the F here is natural, not the F♯ Jason had been playing a moment earlier. Second, the fingering printed in the book is 3–4 and he was using 2–3 — the book's fingering is there so the next shape is reachable.

Then the ending: “basically the last line, plus that last note from the line above”, and on the repeat, “the second time is E, right?” — the second-time bar is different from the first.

Bars 21–23, with the two endings at the finish.
