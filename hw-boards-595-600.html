How it works. Click a piece and its legal squares light up, then click where you want it to go. Got it wrong? Press ↩ Undo and try again.

The board stays quiet at first. It won't say whether a move is right until you've had 10 goes at that puzzle — otherwise you could just click every legal move until one turned green, which isn't solving it. Work the line out in your head, play it, and keep going.

Then you get the lot. Once the 10 goes are used the board starts marking your answers and the whole line appears in notation beside the board — so you can see how it finishes, press ▶ Show the answer to watch it play itself out, or click any move to jump straight there. Only a move from the starting position counts as a go; exploring further down a line is free, and the count is remembered per puzzle.

There's a nudge next to each board if you're stuck — it says how long the mate is, without giving the move away.

A different book. These come from a new one — page 155, numbered 595 to 600 — so they look different from the sheets so far: shaded squares instead of stripes, and no little triangle printed under the board. In this book it is always White to move.

These aren't mates. Every sheet up to now ended in checkmate. This set is about winning material — a piece, or a rook, or enough that the game is over. Four of the six start by giving something away first.

Puzzle 597 has two right first moves, and 599 is the odd one out: no sacrifice at all, just a quiet pawn move. After your 10 goes the full line shows up right here; when Coach covers the set in class this page is rebuilt in the normal open style.
