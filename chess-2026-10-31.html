⭐ Spassky – Fischer, Reykjavik 1972 · game 1

E56 Nimzo-Indian · World Championship match, game 1 · 11 July 1972 · Fischer had Black · 1–0

Three more classes to go this term — the last one is the week before Thanksgiving — and Coach is spending all three on the 1972 World Championship. Last week it was Fischer winning four in a row off Petrosian to earn the match. Now he meets Spassky.

Fischer had never beaten him. Five games before the match: three losses and two draws. “菲舍尔碰到了强大的对手” — Fischer had finally run into a strong opponent.

They argued about the venue, and then about the money. Fischer wanted the Americas or Belgrade; Spassky wanted Reykjavik. FIDE gave it to Reykjavik. The prize fund was $125,000 — enormous for 1972, Coach reckons like $2 million today — and Fischer said it was too little and refused to go.

“如果给我这么多钱，世界冠军赛我去北极比赛都可以。”
For that kind of money I'd play the world championship at the North Pole.
📅 31 Oct 2026 · class, 10:38 — Coach quoting Paul Keres, then world number two

An English businessman then doubled the purse to $250,000 out of his own pocket, and Fischer went. Coach's point: every professional today should thank him for it — that one argument dragged chess prize money up for good. The world championship in a few weeks' time is worth $2.5 million.

Then the demands. An automatic car, a private tennis court and a driver. He turned down a dozen chairs the organisers offered and had the chair he beat Petrosian on in Buenos Aires flown to Iceland, because he won in it.

The game. Spassky had White and got nothing out of the opening — Fischer had a novelty ready on move 14, and the queens came off early. By move 29 it was a dead-drawn bishop ending. And then:

“为什么菲雪尔把这个有毒的兵吃掉了呢？很多人都不解。”
Why did Fischer take that poisoned pawn? Nobody understands it.
📅 31 Oct 2026 · class, 24:30

What he miscalculated. Coach walked it through move by move: Fischer thought the bishop could escape and that White would be left with doubled, isolated pawns. He had even seen the line — he just stopped one move too early and missed that the bishop gets trapped anyway. The annotation on Coach's screen says the same thing: a very rare occurrence in his games.

And then he didn't turn up. Fischer blamed the cameras and the audience noise for the blunder and demanded the match be played with no spectators and no filming. The organisers had a contract with the broadcaster and said no. He didn't appear for game 2 — forfeited, 0–2 down, and bought a ticket home.

What turned it round: the US Secretary of State telephoned him and told him to play — and then Spassky agreed to the empty hall. Coach: many people call that the biggest blunder Spassky made in the whole match, because if he had refused he would simply have won.

“先输两盘……最后大家都知道结果。”
Two down to start with — and everybody knows how it ended.

📖 The King's Gambit — taking the pawn

C33/C39 King's Gambit Accepted · 1.e4 e5 2.f4 · Black's side · the sharp half of a two-week pair

Last week was the quiet answer (…d6, keeping everything closed). This week is the other half: take the pawn. Coach noted that Spassky himself once beat Fischer with the King's Gambit — not in the title match — so it is not a toy.

▶ Live board. The main line, move by move:

The rule for the whole opening: take the pawn, hold it for a moment with …g5 — and then give it back to get your pieces out. Clinging to it is how Black gets into trouble.

“这个兵啊，你不要去保去。你需要的是，快速的出子。”
Don't go defending that pawn. What you need is to get your pieces out, fast.
📅 31 Oct 2026 · class, 35:25

One knight move to remember — and one to avoid. After 4.h4 g4 it has to be 5.Ne5. Play 5.Ng5? and the knight is simply trapped.

▶ Live board. And if White plays 3.Bc4 instead — plus the famous trap not to fall for:

Don't check on h4. 3…Qh4+ looks tempting and it is how the Immortal Game started — but after 4.Kf1 the king is fine and your queen has lost a move. Anderssen won that game with White. Play …Nc6 instead.

Why you see it less and less. Black has too many good answers — take it, play …Bc5, or counter-gambit — and in none of them can White prove anything. “所以碰到王翼弃兵的时候冷静” — so when you meet the King's Gambit, stay calm.

“你自己回去打开 Chess.com、里切斯，里面有 opening……既有英文的讲解，又有具体的变招。”
Go home and open Chess.com or Lichess — there's an openings section, with explanations in English and all the variations. And puzzles on this exact opening.

🎯 What to take away

  • 68 and 69 both hinge on a quiet move. If you only look at checks, you find neither.
  • Go back over the ones you missed and find the calculation error — Coach says that is worth more than the score.
  • Even Fischer miscalculated. He stopped one move too early and lost a bishop in a drawn ending — in the first game of a world championship.
  • In the King's Gambit, give the pawn back. Develop fast; don't defend it.
  • 5.Ne5, never 5.Ng5 — and don't check on h4.
