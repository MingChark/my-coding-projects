How to read a sheet
△ — the little triangle tells you whose move it is. If it's under the board, it's that side to play.
+− — means White is winning. Find the move that proves it.

On the BEST MOVE CONTEST sheets the three rows ask three different questions: MATE (force checkmate), MATERIAL (win a piece or pawn), and BEST MOVE (just find the strongest move). Coach says the Best Move row is the hardest and the one most people guess at.

Where these answers come from: Coach walks through the previous week's homework at the start of every class, out loud. These are his answers, taken from the recordings — not a computer's. Each set says which class to go watch if you want to hear the reasoning.

Puzzles 7–12 Handed out Sat 22 Aug 2026 ✓ Answers below
Tap to open full size

Coach's answers

From the 30 Aug class · recording 03:00–10:30

▶ Play this set on real boards — six separate boards you can move the pieces on, and the answers play out move by move.

  1. Qg8+! — give the queen away. After Kxg8, either Nf6+ (then Nh7#) or Ne7# straight away. Both mate — Coach's point: work out which one you actually want before you play it.
  2. Qe8!
  3. Qxe5 wins — but Rd8! is the better answer. It leads to a queen-sacrifice mate. Coach specifically praised the students who found Rd8, for having "tactical imagination". With Qxe5 the opponent gets an annoying check first.
  4. Nf7 — the easiest of the six.
  5. Qh7! and then Rg5! — the hardest one. The h-file opens by force, the g-pawn can't move, and the king has nowhere to hide. A queen sacrifice followed by a second hard move.
  6. Another queen sacrifice, then mate.

Three students got all six. Coach had deliberately made this set easier than the previous two weeks.

▶ Play this set on real boards — five separate boards you can move the pieces on, with the answers played out. (433 is left out — see the page.)

  1. Qh6!
  2. Qh6! — same square, same idea. Three pieces deliver the mate.
  3. A quiet preparing move first, and then Qh7! You have to open the line before you sacrifice. This was the tricky one.
  4. Qh2! (Black to move here — the triangle is under Black.)
  5. Nb5+! — after cxb5, the other knight mates on b7. Careful: the sheet's own hint points at 1…Nf5?, which is the tempting wrong knight move.

Four students got all six. Coach: the queen sacrifice is "战术组合里面最高级的" — the highest form of tactical combination.

Coach's answers

From the 15 Aug class · recording 01:30–09:00

▶ Play this set on real boards — all six as playable boards, with engine-verified answers.

  1. A queen sacrifice for mate. Refuse it and you're lost anyway; take it and you get mated.
  2. The trap. Ba4 looks like mate — but it's a blunder, because the other side has a check first. Play the knight move first, and then Rd1+ mates. Look for their checks before you commit.
  3. Qg6 — another queen sacrifice. If it's taken, it's double check and mate.
  4. Queen sacrifice, then double check mates.
  5. No queen to give here — so sacrifice something else, then mate.
  6. Qh5 — queen sacrifice again, then mate.

Coach pointed out the first three are all queen sacrifices, and told a story about a student who was brilliant at puzzles but could never solve a queen sacrifice, because he didn't want to give up his queen. "On a chessboard only the king can't be sacrificed."

MATE row

  1. Qg6 — the two bishops finish it off.
  2. Qf6 — same idea, and this time the rook joins in.
  3. Bh6, then the capture, then check. Coach's warning on this one: if you sacrifice a piece, you must also calculate the line where they don't take.

MATERIAL row — his general rule: a pawn sitting on the 5th rank can usually just be taken, let alone a knight. On the third one, Rxf7 deflects the defender and the knight comes in behind it.

BEST MOVE row — Qf3 (taking on f3 with the bishop). Coach: this row is where people guess; the answer came from spotting what was undefended.

🎯 How to get the most out of these

  • Do them before you read the answers. Coach's whole reason for setting them: "do enough puzzles and it becomes familiar — then in a tense game, it comes back to you."
  • Write the answer in notation (like Qg6), not just "the queen goes there". You'll need notation to talk about your games.
  • Before you commit to a pretty move, look for their checks. Puzzle 426 is built entirely around that mistake — and the class fell for it.
  • If you sacrifice, also calculate what happens when they refuse. That's Coach's rule from the Best Move Contest 21 class.
  • Want to move the pieces around instead of just looking? Use the playable board.
